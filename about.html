About us

The people behind IntelliLab

Two of the club websites we studied had no team page at all — you could not tell who ran them or whether they were still active. So we put ours front and centre.

Our mission

To make artificial intelligence approachable and practical for every student on campus — regardless of faculty, coding background or year of study — by teaching through building rather than through slides.

Our vision

A university where every graduate leaves AI-literate: able to use these tools competently, question them critically, and build with them responsibly.

Build first

No session ends without something running. Notebooks, datasets and starter repos are handed out at the door.

Open to everyone

Half our committee started with zero programming background. Beginner tracks run every semester.

Responsible by default

Every technical track includes an ethics component — bias, consent, attribution and environmental cost.

Our story

How we got here

2022

Six people and a borrowed classroom

The society began as an informal reading group working through a machine learning course together on Wednesday evenings.

2023

Officially registered

Recognised as a faculty society with 80 founding members. Our first hackathon drew 12 teams.

2024

The beginner track launches

After feedback that sessions assumed too much, we split the syllabus into Beginner, Intermediate and Advanced streams. Membership tripled.

2025

Industry mentorship

Partnered with four local technology companies to mentor final-year projects and offer internship pathways.

2026 · Now

412 members and counting

Four active tracks, monthly innovation challenges, and a project archive open to the whole university.

Our commitment

Code of ethics

The strongest thing we found in our review of aiEDU was its insistence on responsible AI. We have adopted the same principle as a club rule, not an afterthought.

Any AI-assisted work presented at IntelliLab must declare which tools were used and for what. We treat undisclosed generation as we would treat plagiarism.
Project teams are asked to state who their model could fail for, and to test on data that reflects that group. "We didn't check" is not an acceptable answer at demo night.
No project may train on the personal data of students, staff or the public without documented consent. Face and voice datasets require advisor approval.
We teach compute budgeting: fine-tune before you pre-train, and measure before you scale. Efficiency is part of every project rubric.
Club materials must be readable at 200% zoom, work with a keyboard alone, and avoid colour as the only signal — the same standard this website is built to.
